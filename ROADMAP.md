# Roadmap

Priority order for the open GitHub issues, as of 25 November 2026 (v1.25.0). Effort is a rough guide: **S** is a day or less, **M** a few days, **L** a week or more.

Every open issue is either finished and ready to close, waiting on someone outside (both listed in Tier 0), or has a plan, linked in its row. The plans are local working notes in `docs/` (gitignored). Tier 1 is the work that is next to build; the rest follow in order. Within a tier, cheap items that are ready to build come before ones that still need a decision from Stuart.

| 1 | [456](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/456) | Whole-house figures from a second, read-only inverter (John's AIO + AC-coupled system) | M | John confirmed his wiring on 23 November. The original idea, reading house figures from the Gateway, can't work for him. His solar (about 10.7 kWp on Enphase microinverters), his original AC-coupled inverter with two 9.4 kWh batteries, and the EV charger sit on the main consumer unit, ahead of the Gateway. The house circuits and the AIO are behind it. All three units read the main grid meter, so the Gateway and the AIO miss everything upstream; the Gateway's house reading is 0 whenever the solar or the AC-coupled battery is supplying. The AC-coupled unit already sees the grid and the solar, so the fix is to read it as a second, read-only inverter alongside the AIO HEM controls. Decided on 24 November: the way to read it is a second headless HEM on that inverter, whose snapshot the master pulls, rather than a second Modbus connection inside one HEM; that drops the second connection and poll timing, and a Docker Compose file with both instances would take most of the setup away for John. A direct second connection can come later as another source. The merged figures come from whichever unit measures each one: grid and the import/export totals from the second unit, solar added, and home is solar plus both batteries minus grid. The AIO's own battery, SOC and control fields stay the AIO's so automations never act on the other unit's battery; the second battery rides along in `second_unit`. No new decoding is needed, and it suits any "AC-coupled + hybrid on one supply" install, not just Gateway ones. Decided on 25 November: an optional second-HEM address field in Settings next to the inverter address, empty by default; the master reads the second HEM's main snapshot, not the keyed one; no monitor-only flag for now; and the Planner treats the AC-coupled battery as known load/supply rather than modelling it. Built on 25 November and awaiting a commit and a release (`inverter/second_unit.rs`, the Second HEM Address setting, a status line under it, shown only in Developer Mode for now; verified live against two simulators with a proxy faking the second serial, not yet on John's real pair). To ask John once released: which GE app figure he trusts, and whether HEM can read his EV charger (needed for house-without-the-car). The issue body was rewritten on 25 November around the second-HEM approach (title and text no longer mention the Gateway). John already runs two copies today (the AC-coupled one starts at boot, the AIO one by hand, both managed from his phone) and trusts the AC-coupled figures for house, car and solar, so the second HEM is his existing instance and the Docker Compose file is now optional; he runs both as the Windows desktop app on one PC, and the FAQ now has the steps to start both at boot (posted on the issue 25 November). Plan in `docs/issue-456-gateway-plan.md`, Part 2. |
