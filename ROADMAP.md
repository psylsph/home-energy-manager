# Roadmap

Priority order for the open GitHub issues, as of 7 November 2026 (v1.8.8). Effort is a rough guide: **S** is a day or less, **M** a few days, **L** a week or more.

The order weighs four things: whether users are affected today, whether a later item depends on it, how much it costs, and whether it is waiting on someone else's answer. Cheap items that unblock a user come first, and large or risky items come after the ones that are ready to build. Where it differs from the issue's own label, the reason is given.

## Tier 0: active now

Being built now: [454](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/454), recording car charging separately (Tier 1, order 1; plan in `docs/issue-454-car-charging-plan.md`, all phases built, awaiting review and release). [427](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/427), battery health history following a battery that moves slot, shipped in v1.8.8 and stays open until the reporter confirms it on their two batteries. [452](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/452), the "Open Dongle Page" button for the connection-problem message, shipped in v1.8.7 (see `docs/issue-452-dongle-page-plan.md`) and stays open until the reporter confirms their dongle's page loads on a normal home network, what login it wants, and which dongle generation it is; if it only works on the inverter's own WiFi, the wording changes. The replan idea from the Planner setup thread (discussion #436) is #453 (Tier 5). Items that have shipped are left off; some of those issues stay open until the reporter confirms the fix, and Stuart closes them.

## Tier 1: bugs and user-facing gaps

| Order | # | Issue | Effort | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [454](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/454) | Record car charging separately from home use | L | **Being built (Tier 0).** Today the car is only taken out of the forecast's consumption profile; History, the Power page, the Today bars and the daily report still show it as Home. In a house where only the car pushes load past a known level, any minute above that level is the car at a fixed block (charger amps × grid voltage, about 7.4 kW), worked out when the data is read, so nothing new is stored. It reuses the forecast's "leave out load above" threshold, adds a charger current setting (32 A), is split out on History, Power, Today and the daily report / `/today`, and the forecast profile switches to the same rule so they all agree. It also adds an opt-in "car charging started / finished" notification. Opt-in, and a GivEnergy charger's measured power always wins. Self-contained, so it doesn't wait on anything. Status diagram, house view, cost and MQTT are follow-ups. The detailed plan is in `docs/issue-454-car-charging-plan.md`. |
| 2 | [433](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/433) | Battery pause: offer it on the models GivTCP does | M, then L | GivTCP pauses the Gen1 (ARM 187+, mode only), the Gen2 and Gen3 (below ARM 312) and the AC Mk2 (ARM 200+), which HEM doesn't yet, so Timed Discharge and the Charge Cap are missing there. HEM also gives the AC three-phase a pause that GivTCP doesn't, and the Gen4, three-phase and EMS are on the simulator's pause list when GivTCP has none. Each gap is recorded in `tests/fixtures/givtcp-model-contract.json`, so the tests fail once one is fixed, as the reminder to drop it. The HV Gen3 and AIO Hybrid gained their pause with the single-phase schedule work (v1.8.2), so they are no longer part of this. The Gen1 4xx can't read HR 318 back, so the charge cap and the limiters must cope with no read-back there. |
| 3 | [455](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/455) | Energy Tariffs from Octopus, and the rate burnt into every history row | L, in two halves | Today History costs and the daily report price every past minute with the tariff as it is now, so changing a rate (or fixing a typo) silently rewrites last month. The first half stamps each history row with the import rate, export rate and standing charge in force when it was recorded, falling back to the current tariff for older rows, with an explicit "re-price from a date" action and an exact backfill for Octopus users (the supplier's rates come with dates). That half is self-contained and should ship first. The second half fills Energy Tariffs from the Octopus rates HEM already downloads (import and export windows, standing charge, and a new gas rate and standing charge), only when the tariff fits fixed daily windows, so Agile stays manual; it is safer once rows carry their own price because a rate change then only moves the future. Open questions (opt-in or automatic, where it sits in Settings, how to treat Intelligent Octopus Go's off-peak dispatch rate) are in the plan. Start after #454 lands, since both touch the history row writer. The detailed plan is in `docs/issue-455-octopus-tariffs-plan.md`. |

## Tier 2: smarter planning around Intelligent Octopus Go

These come from the #403 discussion, where Brian and Stuart split the original thread into separate tickets. They share one theme (the planner should know what the car and the Octopus dispatch list are doing). #404 comes first because it is the smaller, ready piece. #434 is the exception: it builds on the #431 hold work, which has shipped but is waiting for the reporter to confirm it on their AIO, so it moves up as soon as they do. The export-advice pair from the same thread (#407, #405) is larger and riskier, so it sits in Tier 4, after the work that protects and widens the user base.

| Order | # | Issue | Effort | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [404](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/404) | Re-plan and notify when Octopus moves a dispatch slot | M | Octopus keeps moving slots through the evening, so a plan sized against an old slot goes stale. It reuses the dispatch-as-load handling from #406 and the existing "what the automatic plan did" reporting. The ownership rules for the shared Eco Paused state must keep winning over a running smart charge. |
| 2 | [434](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/434) | Charge the battery during Intelligent Octopus Go smart charging | M | An opt-in on the Octopus page with a target SOC, reusing the Force Charge path and the hold's ownership rules. Open questions are in the issue: a supply cap (about 11.5 kW was seen with the car and one AIO charging), and which dispatches count. Start once the reporter has confirmed the #431 hold on their AIO, so the hold is proven first. |

## Tier 4: Planner export advice

| Order | # | Issue | Effort | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [407](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/407) | Planner minimum battery level as an overridable floor | M | The reporter has said what they meant: a back-stop that stops optimised exporting from emptying the battery before the cheap window, tightened as HEM learns the household's evening discharge rate. That is a guard for #405 rather than a change to the existing floor, so design it with #405 and don't ship it alone. The original post is still ambiguous; confirm it with the reporter before building. |
| 2 | [405](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/405) | Export surplus charge before the cheap window | L | The riskiest item here. The simulation it needs already exists as the Planner's export advice (`plan_export_window`), which projects hourly solar and consumption to the next cheap import window — but it sizes a duration, and a Saving Session's window is Octopus's, so this needs the inverse: the floor that leaves the house covered until 23:30. #446 (shipped in v1.8.4) built the thing it writes to (a held Minimum SOC during a window), #407 is the back-stop for a thin projection; it also builds on the dispatch-as-load handling from #406 and cannot export while the car is charging. Start as Planner advice (as the other export advice is) and consider acting on its own only after real-world use. |

## Tier 5: later

| Order | # | Issue | Effort | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [398](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/398) | Tariff comparison: what would last year have cost? | L | A popular question. It no longer waits on #132: it prices against the public Octopus product rates, with the existing tariff editor supplying the "what I actually paid" baseline. Start by re-pricing actual import and export, then re-run the planner's simulation per tariff. |
| 2 | [453](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/453) | Planner: plan a charge in every cheap period, not just the cheapest | L | Split from #448. The Planner and its automatic refresh each pick one window, so a tariff like Cosy's three cheap periods gets one charge a day. Planning each period means telling the planner which window it is for, firing the refresh once per period, and rethinking how the three strategies and the recurring charge slot behave. The design pass is written up: a toggle that is off by default keeps existing installs unchanged, and checking more often would not help on its own because the limits are the once-a-day latch and the single-window pick. The open questions are settled (same-rate windows, skip a late window, toggle beside the strategy selector, slot 1 only), so it is ready to build. Cosy mode already charges in every window, so the cost of waiting is low. The detailed plan is in the `docs` directory (`docs/issue-453-every-cheap-window-plan.md`). |

| 4 | [392](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/392) | Import history from the GivEnergy Cloud | L | Needs a GivEnergy Premium subscription, and the 402 for a free key hasn't been confirmed. | 5 | [132](https://github.com/psylsph/home-energy-manager/issues/132) | Energy Tariffs: don't hard-code 00:00/23:59 | M | Moved to the bottom on purpose. Only one person has asked for it, and it changes how tariffs are stored and validated, which feeds History costs, the planner and the tariff automations, so a mistake could break existing installs. If it is picked up, keep it additive: existing saved tariffs must load and price exactly as before, new options such as a base rate with override windows or windows that cross midnight must be opt-in, and the old and new forms need tests that compare costs side by side. Revisit if more people ask. |
